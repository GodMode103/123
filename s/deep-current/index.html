−200 m · The Twilight Zone begins

The last of the sunlight gives up here

Photosynthesis ends at this line. Everything below either swims up to eat, waits for food to fall, or learns to make its own light. Ninety percent of the ocean lies beneath this sentence.

PRESSURE: 21 atmospheres — a submarine hull begins to matter.

−1,000 m · The Midnight Zone

Where the animals invented lanterns

Bioluminescence is the most common form of communication on Earth — most of the planet's creatures live down here, speaking in flashes. The lights you see drifting past are how the dark says hello, threatens, courts, and lies.

76% of deep-sea species produce their own light. Evolution invented it at least 94 separate times.

−3,800 m · The Abyssal Plain

A famous ship rests at this depth

The Titanic sits in cold this absolute — about 1°C — under pressure that would crush a car into a drink can. Yet life carpets the seafloor: sea cucumbers, glass sponges, and bacteria eating the ship itself, half a ton of it a day.

MARINE SNOW: the dead falling from above — the only weather, and the only food economy, down here.

−6,000 m · The Hadal Zone

Named after Hades, on purpose

Only the trenches go deeper than this. Fish stop here — below ~8,400 m their cells can no longer hold shape against the pressure. What continues down is stranger: amphipods the size of house cats, and creatures we have photographed exactly once.

More people have walked on the Moon than have reached the deepest trench floor.

−10,935 m · Challenger Deep

You are at the bottom of the world

If Everest were dropped into this trench, its summit would still be two kilometers underwater. The pressure here is 1,086 times the surface — and something is alive in every photograph we have ever taken of it.
